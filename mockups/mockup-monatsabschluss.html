Reading this mockup: two very different cell types, by design. Blue "auto" items show a live ✓/⚠ that you never touch by hand — it's just the app's existing checks (Außenstände net-zero, the Barkonten/Rücklagen reconciliation, Verlauf's ok-switches) surfaced here; hovering/tapping a ⚠ (like September's "AIRBUS Reisekosten," reflecting 2026-05 HAM's real −336,50 € open balance) tells you exactly what's unresolved, and clicking would jump straight to that screen. Plain-labeled items stay real, tappable checkboxes — the genuine manual to-dos, including every per-account Daten item, confirmed still needed since most of those accounts have no CSV export or too few monthly bookings to be worth automating. Past months (Jan–Aug) are shown fully checked/green to illustrate a normal closed history, except one deliberately planted warning back in June (Consors Verrechnungskonto) to show that a past month can still surface a problem if something changes retroactively. The current month (September, highlighted column) is where real interaction happens; future months are visibly disabled. "Tagesgeld kopieren" is flagged as manual-for-now per the spec's open item — its exact binding still needs clarifying once we know precisely what it copies. On phone, the grid becomes a single-month list, navigated with ‹ ›, same auto/manual distinction carried through.
